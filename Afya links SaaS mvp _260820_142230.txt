AFYA LINKS — MULTI-PHARMACY WHOLESALE ORDERING PLATFORM
1. PRODUCT OBJECTIVE
Build a centralized SaaS platform owned and controlled by Afya Links that connects:
Clinics → Wholesale Pharmacies → Payment
The system does not maintain a medicine catalogue, inventory, or pharmacy pricing database.
Instead:
1. Each wholesale pharmacy provides a dedicated WhatsApp number (to act as the Clinic-Facing Bot) AND their personal/staff WhatsApp number (for receiving notifications).
2. Afya Links connects the Clinic-Facing Bot number through Baileys. Afya Links also maintains ONE Central Platform WhatsApp number.
3. The Clinic-Facing number becomes the pharmacy's dedicated ordering channel for clinics.
4. Clinics send orders directly to that Clinic-Facing WhatsApp number.
5. Afya Links (via Gemini AI) receives and records the order.
6. The Afya Links Central Platform Number automatically forwards the order details (with an Order ID) to the pharmacy's personal/staff WhatsApp number.
7. The pharmacy staff reviews the order and replies to the Central Platform Number with the Order ID and the final total amount (e.g., "ORD-123 50000").
8. The system detects this reply, updates the order, and creates a payment request using that pharmacy's PesaPal credentials.
9. The Clinic-Facing Bot number sends the branded PesaPal payment link back to the clinic.
10. Clinic pays.
11. Money is processed through the pharmacy's own PesaPal merchant account.
12. Afya Links receives the payment status and updates the order, notifying both parties.
13. Afya Links Super Admin can monitor the entire transaction.

Do not build one giant WhatsApp session.

text

├── Session: pharmacy_001
│     └── Abacus number
│
├── Session: pharmacy_002
│     └── XYZ number
│
└── Session: pharmacy_003
      └── ABC number

Each session must be independently:
• authenticated
• connected
• disconnected
• reconnected
• monitored
• logged
There are existing multi-session Baileys implementations demonstrating this architecture, but for your own MVP, keep the session manager inside your backend rather than depending on an unknown third-party wrapper.

33. RLS
Use Supabase Row Level Security.
Even though the Super Admin backend needs broad access, don't simply expose your entire database to the frontend.
Supabase recommends RLS for granular row-level access and specifically warns that service-role/secret keys bypass RLS and must remain server-side.

And the business logic is:
The pharmacy's WhatsApp number determines the pharmacy.
The pharmacy determines the final price.
The pharmacy's PesaPal credentials determine where the payment transaction is processed.
Afya Links controls the software and records the transaction.
The clinic doesn't need an Afya Links account.
The pharmacy doesn't need an Afya Links dashboard in the MVP.
The public payment experience uses getafyalinks.com, not Railway.
That is the MVP I would build first. It is substantially narrower than a full pharmacy-management platform, which is exactly why you can get a working version into production much faster.
